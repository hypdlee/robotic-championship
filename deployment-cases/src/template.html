Ward deployment cases: people, alerts and power

Eleven scripted hospital situations for a robot towing a trolley. Each one runs the Chapter 5 safety layer (the six danger states, the alert chain) and the battery power supervisor, so you can watch who is where, what the layer decided, and what the robot did.

These are simulations, not recordings. The people, their paths and the moments the camera misses them are scripted for each case. The danger ladder, time constants and version differences come from Chapter 5. The robot slowing down in response to alerts, the LiDAR layout, the map and the planner are extensions proposed here: the chapter's layer only warns. Switch them off below to see the layer as deployed.

Sensor layout: what we have and what we propose

What the LiDAR adds (cases 10 and 11 are new, the others improve)

    What still needs care

    • The rear 44 degrees and everything behind the trolley stay blind: no reversing while towing.
    • Glass is invisible to the LiDAR, so a glass partition is not in the map and cannot veto a camera ghost. Mark glass walls as keep-out zones.
    • Objects lower than 0.29 m in the first 1.3 m ahead rely on the camera tilt and the stop sensor.
    • Everything here is simulated. The LiDAR model, the planner and the SLAM map are simple rules, not a real ROS 2 stack.

    All nine cases as an acceptance suite

    Each case is a pass or fail test with a stated criterion. A new version of the safety layer should pass all of them before it goes near a ward. The earlier versions fail exactly the cases that exposed their bugs in the chapter. The right-hand block shows the same tests with the proposed LiDAR layout.

    How these cases go onto a ward

    1. Simulation (this page). All nine cases pass on the version you plan to deploy.
    2. Bench drills. Staff volunteers stand at marked distances (0.78 m, 1.07 m, 1.62 m, as in the chapter's figures) with the robot's wheels off the ground. The states must match the ladder.
    3. Ward shadow mode. The layer runs during normal work and only logs. Count false alerts per hour and unflagged close approaches. Nothing is commanded.
    4. Advisory. Spoken and visual alerts on, staff briefed that the stop button always works.
    5. Motion response. Slowing or stopping for alerts is a separate, separately approved pilot.

    What this page does not cover

    • Real YOLO behaviour. Misses, the dark and the 0.3 m blind zone are scripted rules, not a model.
    • The LSTM intent classifier and the shoulder-keypoint facing check. Only the distance and speed rules of Table 5.1 are modelled.
    • Assumed here, not in the chapter: a 1.5 s prediction horizon, CAUTION only within 3 m, an 80 degree camera field of view, and the speed caps used when the robot responds.
    • Spoken phrases other than "Move away immediately" and "Please clear the path" are illustrative.
    • Crowds, beds, wheelchairs, lifts and doors, and any person behind the robot or outside the camera view.
